How I Placed in the Top 750 of 10,000+ in My First Hackathon

November 2026

A few months ago, I had a problem most people would envy: free time. I had just started my first job, my evenings were empty, and I had no idea what to do with them.

So I did what I usually do when I'm stuck. I opened a chat with Claude and started thinking out loud. What should I be doing with my life? Where are the people who care about computer science as much as I do?

That second question mattered more than I realized. I didn't just want something to do. I wanted to be around builders.

That's when I found AI Seekho 2026.

Signing up without a plan

I had never entered a hackathon before. I didn't have a team, a pitch deck, or a polished idea. I signed up anyway, because waiting until I felt ready would have meant never signing up at all.

From blank page to Interior-AI

The hardest part wasn't the code. It was the idea.

I went through idea after idea, throwing most of them away, until one stuck: Interior-AI, a tool that uses AI to help people design their interiors. It was specific, it was useful, and most importantly, I could actually build it.

So I built it. I shipped it. I submitted it.

The result

More than 10,000 people applied. I finished in the top 750, and Google sent me swag to prove it.

It wasn't first place. But for a 15-year-old's first hackathon, entered on a whim from Karachi, it felt like a win. In every way that mattered, it was one.

What I took away

You don't need to be ready. You need to start. A boring evening and one honest conversation led me to my first hackathon, my first shipped AI product, and a community of people who think like me.

This was the first one. It won't be the last.
